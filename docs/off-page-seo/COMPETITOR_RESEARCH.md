# Competitor research

Date checked: 12 November 2026  
Method: public web pages and the GitHub repository page for Cobalt.  
Not used: Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz, Majestic, or any other backlink index.

## Data that is unavailable

The following were **not collected**, because this workspace has no access to a backlink index and because copying scores from other blogs would present someone else's model as a measurement:

- Referring domains for any competitor
- Individual backlink URLs pointing at competitors
- Domain Authority, Domain Rating, Authority Score, or spam score
- Organic keyword counts, rankings, or traffic
- Which of a competitor's links are dofollow

Any later row that gains one of those figures must name the tool, the date, and the URL that was looked up. Until then the tracker column `Domain authority` stays blank.

Comparison articles also disagree with each other about platform counts, maximum quality, and ads. Those figures are vendor or reviewer claims. They are not ReelFetch test results. Do not repeat them in outreach.

## How competitors were chosen

A competitor here means a public tool a person might use instead of ReelFetch to save a public social or web video. Selection used pages that were opened or returned by search on 12 November 2026. Inclusion is not an endorsement, and it is not a claim that the site is safe.

### Open-source web app

**Cobalt** — https://cobalt.tools/ and https://github.com/imputnet/cobalt

The project page describes a media downloader for publicly accessible video, audio, photos, and GIFs, and says it does not use ads, trackers, or paywalls. The repository states that the end user is responsible for what they download and that Cobalt does not cache content. On 12 November 2026 the GitHub page displayed 46,120 stars. That is a public popularity count, not a backlink metric.

Cobalt's own API readme lists support notes per service, including public Facebook video, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, X, Reddit, and Pinterest, plus services ReelFetch does not document (for example Vimeo, SoundCloud, Bluesky). Treat that list as Cobalt's documentation, not as a promise that every URL works.

**yt-dlp** — command-line project, not a hosted website. Relevant because many web downloaders, including ReelFetch's backend, are wrappers or cousins of that approach. People comparing "install a program" versus "use a site" are part of this category. Do not pitch yt-dlp users with a commercial guest post.

### Long-standing multi-site websites named in current comparison articles

These names appear in public 2026 comparison pages. Their live domains, ownership, and safety were **not** audited by opening every homepage:

- SaveFrom.net, discussed as an older general-purpose downloader. One comparison says the web version is ad-heavy and that availability varies by country. Not verified here.
- Sites still using the Y2Mate name. One 2026 comparison page claims the original y2mate.com was affected by an IFPI action in October 2025 and that later sites are copies. That claim was **not** independently checked. Do not send users to an unverified lookalike domain, and do not cite it as a linking partner.
- 9xbuddy, named in a comparison list. Not opened for this research.

### Single-platform sites named in the same articles

SnapTik, SSSTik / SSSTikTok, SnapInsta, and FBDown are repeatedly described as TikTok, Instagram, or Facebook specialists. Their current feature claims were not retested. They matter because searchers often want one platform, which is why ReelFetch's platform guides are the right link targets.

### Desktop apps

4K Video Downloader, ClipGrab, and Stacher show up when people would rather install software. They are indirect competitors. A directory that only lists downloadable desktop software is a weak fit for a website.

## What their public positioning shows

Without a backlink crawl, the observable gap is editorial, not "who links to them."

| Pattern on public comparison pages | ReelFetch position that is more precise |
| --- | --- |
| Wide platform counts and "HD" or "4K" as a blanket claim | Quality is whatever that URL returns. Full HD is not guaranteed. |
| Little room for copyright | A dedicated copyright and takedown page, plus FAQ language that private media is out of scope |
| "No install" versus a store app | ReelFetch is a website. The PWA is an installed website, not an App Store or APK product |
| Privacy claims that conflict with ad scripts | The privacy page names Microsoft Clarity and Google AdSense |
| One generic homepage for every platform | Separate guides for seven platforms, each with its own steps and limits |

Those are the pages to offer when a relevant article needs a source. They are not proof that anyone has linked yet.

## Link-building opportunities that follow from the gaps

These are ideas, not discovered backlinks.

1. **Alternatives pages on real directories.** AlternativeTo and SaaSHub exist so people can compare tools. A careful listing that names Cobalt and desktop apps as alternatives is more credible than a listing that claims to replace every downloader.
2. **Explainers, not "top 10 downloaders" posts.** The category is crowded with listicles. A stronger pitch is a permission checklist or a plain description of how a browser saves a file. Drafts are in `GUEST_POST_STRATEGY.md`.
3. **Creator and educator audiences.** Teachers, journalists, and creators who need a copy of their own public video are a better audience than pages that teach people to ignore platform rules.
4. **Do not pursue mirror-domain networks.** Downloader brands often spawn many domains. A link from a network of thin domains would look manipulative even if someone offered it.

## What to monitor later

See the workflow at the end of `OFF_PAGE_SEO_STRATEGY.md` and the monthly template. When a backlink tool is available, the first exports to request are:

- New referring domains to cobalt.tools, savefrom.net, and one current single-platform specialist, limited to editorial pages.
- Referring domains that link to at least two of those sites and also publish original writing.
- Anchor text that is only "click here to download" or a commercial exact match. Those pages are usually poor targets.

Discard anything that is a link exchange footer, a casino or pharma site, a comment, or a page that exists only to list downloaders.
